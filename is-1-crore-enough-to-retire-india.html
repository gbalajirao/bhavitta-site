Is ₹1 crore enough to retire in India?

What ₹1 crore can pay each month if you stop at 45, 50, 55 or 60, what a small pension adds, and what each city's cost of living does to it. Every figure from the engine, in rupees.

Short answer. For most couples, not on its own. ₹1 crore supports about ₹25,000 a month from 60 with an 80% chance of lasting to 90, ₹21,000 from 55 and ₹15,000 from 45, in today's money, with health insurance paid on top. On ₹40,000 a month it lasts in 41% of market paths from 60. A fixed ₹15,000-a-month pension lifts that to 66% and raises the safe figure to about ₹34,000.

"Is ₹1 crore enough to retire?" was the classic Indian retirement number for a generation, and it still comes up constantly. It was a lot of money when the question was first asked. The tables below come from Bhavitta's own engine, the same one the app runs: 10,000 simulated market paths for each figure, in rupees, for a couple who stop working at 45, 50, 55 or 60 and need the money to last to 90.

What the numbers assume

  • A couple with no further income (except in the pension table). The corpus is everything they hold the day they stop working.
  • Monthly spending in today's rupees, rising with Bangalore's inflation of 5% a year (state CPI-Urban). Health insurance comes on top: ₹40,000 a year today, rising 10% a year.
  • Returns from the app's Balanced India Monte Carlo preset: 9.5% a year on average, 13% volatility, all in rupees. 10,000 trials per figure.
  • Survival means the balance stays above zero in every year to 90, not just at the end. 80% is the bar the app uses for "ready".
  • EPF, PPF and NPS count inside the corpus. If part of it is locked until 58 or 60, the liquid part has to carry you until then.
  • No tax in retirement. Add a tax rate to the retirement stage in the app if your withdrawals will be taxed.
  • City tables use the app's resident cost of living for a comfortable couple, including rent, and each city's own inflation rate, instead of a monthly figure.

What changes the answer most

  • Owning your home. At this budget rent can take a large share of the spending. Without it, ₹25,000 a month covers far more.
  • A pension or rent. Any income that arrives every month shortens the stretch the corpus has to cover alone. One that rises with prices is worth much more than a fixed one.
  • Health cover. The premium rising 10% a year is a large share of a ₹1 crore budget. Employer or government cover that continues after you stop work changes the picture.
  • Working a few more years. Each year adds to the corpus and takes a year off the stretch it has to last; stopping at 60 instead of 55 adds about ₹4,000 a month.
  • Where you live. The corpus-by-city guide gives the number each city needs, and retiring in a metro, then moving shows what a later move saves.

Common questions

Is ₹1 crore enough to retire in India?

For most couples, no. ₹1 crore supports about ₹25,000 a month from 60 with an 80% chance of lasting to 90, plus health insurance, and less if you stop earlier. A typical big-city cost of living for a couple, ₹64,500 to ₹1,07,500 a month including rent, needs two to four times as much.

How much monthly income can ₹1 crore give?

About ₹25,000 a month in today's money, rising with inflation, for a couple from 60; ₹21,000 from 55, ₹18,000 from 50 and ₹15,000 from 45. Those keep an 80% chance of the money lasting to 90, with health insurance paid on top.

Is ₹1 crore enough to retire at 60?

Only on very modest spending. On ₹30,000 a month it lasts to 90 in 70% of paths, on ₹40,000 in 41%. With a fixed ₹15,000 monthly pension, ₹40,000 a month reaches 66% and the safe level rises to about ₹34,000.

Is ₹1 crore enough to retire at 45?

No, unless you spend very little: it supports about ₹15,000 a month from 45 at an 80% chance. On ₹30,000 a month it lasts in 40% of paths, on ₹50,000 in 7%.

How much do I need to retire on ₹50,000 a month?

About ₹2 crore if you stop at 55 and ₹1.75 crore at 60, for an 80% chance of lasting to 90, with health insurance on top. ₹40,000 a month needs ₹1.65 crore and ₹1.45 crore.

Is ₹1 crore enough for a single person?

These figures are for a couple's monthly spending, but the engine only sees the spending, so a single person spending the same amount gets the same answer. On ₹25,000 a month from 60, ₹1 crore has an 80% chance; enter your own figure in the app to check yours.
