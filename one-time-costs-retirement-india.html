College, weddings, cars: how one-time costs change your corpus

A child's college, a wedding, two cars and home repairs, alone and together, and what each adds to the corpus a couple at 55 needs. Every figure from the engine, in rupees.

By Balaji Gururajan · Published 7 November 2026 · Reflects Bhavitta 2.9 · 5 min read

Short answer. Big one-time costs add to the corpus almost rupee for rupee, and together they add up fast. For a Bangalore couple retiring at 55, a child's last four years of college (₹8 lakh a year) add about ₹30 lakh to the corpus they need, a wedding at 60 (₹20 lakh today) about ₹20 lakh, two cars about ₹25 lakh and a round of home repairs about ₹15 lakh. All four together lift the corpus from ₹3.05 crore to ₹3.95 crore; without the extra ₹90 lakh, the chance of lasting to 90 falls from 81% to 53%.

Most retirement calculators ask for monthly spending and stop there. Indian retirements often carry a few large, lumpy costs on top: the end of a child's education, a wedding, a car or two, a house that needs work. They are easy to leave out because they are not "spending", and each one looks affordable on its own. The tables below add them to a plan one at a time and then together, using the app's engine: 10,000 simulated market paths, in rupees.

Enter the price in the year you pay it

A one-time cost in Bhavitta is entered as the amount you will pay in that year, not today's price. A ₹20 lakh wedding five years from now costs about ₹25.5 lakh then at 5% inflation; a ₹12 lakh car bought 15 years from now costs about ₹24.9 lakh. The figures above use those grown amounts: ₹25.5 lakh for the wedding, ₹15.3 lakh and ₹24.9 lakh for the cars and ₹24.4 lakh for the repairs. College is different: it is a yearly cost entered in today's money, and the app grows it with inflation itself.

The further away the cost, the more this matters. Entering today's ₹20 lakh for a wedding five years away moves this plan by about one point; for a car fifteen years away, today's price would be about half the real one.

What the numbers assume

  • A couple retiring at 55 in Bangalore with no further income, at the app's resident cost of living (₹9.7 lakh a year today, rising 5% a year), with health insurance at ₹40,000 a year rising 10%.
  • The costs: college fees of ₹8 lakh a year in today's money for four years from 55; a wedding at 60, cars at 60 and 70 and home repairs at 65, each at today's price grown 5% a year to the year it is paid.
  • Returns from the app's Balanced India Monte Carlo preset: 9.5% a year on average, 13% volatility. 10,000 trials per figure.
  • Survival means the balance stays above zero every year to 90. 80% is the bar the app uses for "ready".

Common questions

How do I plan for my child's wedding in my retirement corpus?

Add it as its own cost in the year you expect to pay, at that year's price. For a Bangalore couple retiring at 55, a wedding costing ₹20 lakh today and paid at 60 (about ₹25.5 lakh then) adds about ₹20 lakh to the corpus they need for an 80% chance of lasting to 90.

Should children's education be part of the retirement corpus?

If the fees fall after you stop working, yes. Four years of college at ₹8 lakh a year from the first year of retirement adds about ₹30 lakh to the corpus in this example, and costs 9 points of survival on ₹3.05 crore.

How much extra corpus do I need for big expenses in retirement?

Roughly their total in today's money. College, a wedding, two cars and home repairs come to about ₹90 lakh at today's prices in this example, and the corpus needed rises by ₹90 lakh, from ₹3.05 crore to ₹3.95 crore.

Does it matter when a one-time cost happens?

Less than you might expect for a single cost. A wedding at 56 and one at 70 each leave this plan at 75%: the later one costs more rupees but comes out of a pot that has had longer to grow. What matters more is the total, and remembering to include them at all.
