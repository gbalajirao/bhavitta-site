Health insurance in retirement: how much does it add to your corpus?

What a premium rising 10% or 13% a year adds to the corpus you need at 45 or 55, and what it costs at 65, 75 and 85. Every figure from the engine, in rupees.

By Balaji Gururajan · Published 5 November 2026 · Reflects Bhavitta 2.9 · 5 min read

Short answer. For a couple retiring at 55 in Bangalore, a ₹40,000 health-insurance premium that rises 10% a year adds about ₹30 lakh to the corpus they need: ₹3.05 crore instead of ₹2.75 crore for an 80% chance of lasting to 90. If premiums rise 13% a year it adds ₹45 lakh; a ₹80,000 premium adds ₹55 lakh. Retiring at 45, the same three cost ₹35 lakh, ₹75 lakh and ₹70 lakh. By 85 the premium is about ₹7 lakh a year, 14% of the couple's spending, up from 4% at 55.

Health cover is the expense that grows fastest in an Indian retirement. Employer group cover ends when the job does, a family floater has to be bought and renewed at retirement-age prices, and premiums rise faster than general inflation because both medical costs and your age push them up. Most retirement calculators fold health into a single inflation rate. Bhavitta models it as its own yearly cost with its own growth rate, so the tables below show what it adds on its own. Every figure is from the app's engine: 10,000 simulated market paths, in rupees.

For the corpus guide's Bangalore couple, health insurance is 4% of yearly spending at 55, 8% at 70 and 14% at 85. A plan that looks comfortable at 60 can be squeezed at 80 by this one line.

What the numbers assume

  • A couple with no further income, retiring at 45 or 55 in Bangalore at the app's resident cost of living (₹9.7 lakh a year today, rising 5% a year).
  • Health insurance as a separate yearly cost from the day they retire: ₹40,000 today by default, rising 10% a year. Industry surveys put Indian medical inflation at 10–13% a year.
  • Returns from the app's Balanced India Monte Carlo preset: 9.5% a year on average, 13% volatility. 10,000 trials per figure.
  • Survival means the balance stays above zero in every year to 90. 80% is the bar the app uses for "ready".
  • No claims, co-pays or uncovered bills. The premium is the only health cost here. Out-of-pocket costs come on top, and a one-time bill can be added in the app as a life event.

Common questions

How much does health insurance add to my retirement corpus in India?

For a couple retiring at 55 in Bangalore, a ₹40,000 premium rising 10% a year adds about ₹30 lakh to the corpus needed for an 80% chance of lasting to 90. Retiring at 45 it adds about ₹35 lakh. Faster-rising or larger premiums add ₹45–75 lakh.

How fast do health insurance premiums rise in India?

Industry surveys put Indian medical inflation at about 10–13% a year, and premiums also step up with age bands. The app uses 10% a year by default; you can change it on the health-insurance item in your plan.

What will my health insurance premium be at 80?

A ₹40,000 premium at 55 rising 10% a year is about ₹4.3 lakh a year at 80 in that year's money, or roughly ₹1.3 lakh in today's money after 5% general inflation. At 13% a year it would be about ₹2.5 lakh in today's money.

Does employer health insurance continue after retirement?

Group cover through an employer usually ends when employment ends, so a retiree needs individual or family cover bought at their age. Buying before retirement, while still healthy, avoids fresh waiting periods on existing conditions. Check your own policy's terms.

Should health insurance be part of the retirement corpus calculation?

Yes, as its own line. A premium rising 10% a year is four times its starting size in today's money by 85; folded into 5% general inflation it would stay flat. Modelled separately, it adds ₹30–75 lakh to the corpus in these examples.
